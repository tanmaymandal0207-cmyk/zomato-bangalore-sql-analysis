Popular isn't loved

165 of the city's 1,942 most-voted restaurants are rated below 3.5. Compared with the well-rated restaurants around them, they are more likely to take online orders, more likely to be chain outlets, and twice as likely to be priced ₹301–600 for two. This page shows where they cluster and where to act first.

Scope: established restaurants (votes above the 242 average of voted restaurants, rated) Low-rated: rating below 3.5 Peers: established, rated 3.5+ (1,777) Grain: one row per restaurant, latest snapshot
Business problem

Discovery on Zomato leans on popularity. These 165 restaurants still hold 67,362 votes of customer attention while customers rate them 2.93 on average, against 4.11 for their peers.

Question

What do popular, low-rated restaurants have in common, and is the problem the brand or the individual outlet?

Decision it supports

Where the restaurant-partner team should spend a quality-improvement programme first, and which outlets to put on the list.

The mid-price trap

Share of established restaurants rated below 3.5, by price for two

106 of the 165 are priced ₹301–600. In that band 16.3% of popular restaurants are low-rated, against 0.8% at ₹1,000+.

What to do

Recommendations, each sized from the data above

1

Fix outlets, not brands, where sister outlets are healthy

Run outlet audits benchmarked against the same brand's best outlets: same menu and supply chain, so the gap is execution (kitchen, packaging, staff).

2

Put the ₹301–600 band on a watchlist

Coach Quick Bites in this band to compete on value and Casual Dining to compete on experience. Mid-priced restaurants that do neither are the ones customers mark down.

3

Test the delivery leg

Online-ordering restaurants are much more likely to be low-rated. Check delivery times, packaging and order accuracy for the online outlets on the list before blaming the food.

4

Weight discovery by quality, start with the priority list

Blend rating into ranking so votes alone don't keep these outlets on top. Work the list in vote order: the most customers are exposed at the top.

Method and caveats

  • Source: BigQuery restaurants_clean (51,717 listing rows) reduced to 12,453 restaurants (URL path; highest-vote listing kept).
  • Established: votes above 242.08 (the average of the 9,446 restaurants with at least one vote) and rated: 1,942 restaurants = 165 low-rated + 1,777 peers.
  • Chain: brand name with 5+ outlets city-wide (exact name match). Format: first value of rest_type.
  • Ranges: 95% normal-approximation intervals on the difference in proportions. Price cells with fewer than 15 restaurants are hidden.
  • These are associations, not causes. Votes are not orders, and ratings are one snapshot.
  • The findings hold at other cut-offs: at a rating below 3.3 or 3.7, low-rated restaurants are still 81–84% online and 42–49% chain, against about 66% and 25% for peers.
  • One residual duplicate sits in the list (Biryani Junction, Bommanahalli, two URLs with 298 votes each), so there are 164 unique outlets. It is flagged in the priority list.
  • 8 established restaurants have no listed price; they count in the KPIs but not in the price charts.
